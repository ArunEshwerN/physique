How this page works

The four tiles above are the only thing you need to look at before a session. They tell you which band mark to use, how many reps to do, and how far into the rung you are.

After every session you log clean or not clean. Clean means every rep hit the standard — chin clearly over the bar, armpits clapped, no swing, no kipping, no fifth rep scraped through for the sake of it. Two clean sessions clear a stage. Three stages clear a rung. One bad rep anywhere and the session is not clean, and you repeat it. That is the entire method.

The projected date moves with your real pace. It starts as an estimate. After your first cleared rung it stops estimating and starts measuring — so if you take nine sessions on a rung instead of six, the date moves out honestly rather than quietly lying to you. Your data lives in this browser only; nothing is sent anywhere.

Non-negotiable

The rules

  1. Every rung gets cleared twice at every rep stage. Six clean sessions minimum per rung. This was your call and it is the right one — the failure mode you described on Mark 10 is exactly what single-clearance ladders produce.
  2. The rep stages are 5/4/4 → 5/5/4 → 5/5/5. Then drop a mark and go straight back to 5/4/4. You never carry reps across a band change.
  3. One cue only: clap the armpits. Never swap cues mid-set, never stack two. Boring is the point.
  4. A scraped rep voids the session. Not a punishment — information. You logged it, you repeat it, you move on.
  5. Three sessions a week, Monday / Wednesday / Friday. If you miss one, shift forward. Never do two pull-up sessions on consecutive days.
  6. If a rung takes more than ten sessions, don't grind it — go back up one mark and re-clear it. You lose two weeks and gain the rep quality that was missing.
  7. Pain stops the session. Muscle burning is fine. Anything sharp in the elbow, shoulder or wrist ends it that day, no debate.
On the eleven-day break

You noticed the cue took a few sets to come back and the strength was still there. That's exactly right and it's worth knowing permanently: after a layoff, the skill returns before the strength is ever lost. A week or two off costs you a session or two of re-learning, not weeks of progress. Never panic-restart after a break — re-enter at the same rung and the same reps.

The habit change — this matters more than the routine

Do this

Take the Tall Standing Reset out of the routine and into your day. Three times daily, twenty seconds, whenever you remember — waiting for a kettle, standing in a lift, before you sit down at your desk. Keep it in the routine too.

Posture is not a strength problem, it's a default position problem. Ten minutes on a mat teaches your body the shape; standing in that shape sixty times a week is what makes it the one you fall into without thinking. The mat work alone will plateau. This is the part that transfers.

Short version, when time is tight

  • Pelvic tilts — 10
  • Glute bridges — 2 × 10
  • Hip flexor stretch — 30 s per side
  • Tall standing reset — 2 × 20 s

That's four minutes and it is genuinely enough on a bad day.

Do you keep it once the main program starts?

Yes, but it shrinks. Once you're on Phase 0 and then Upper/Lower, the main program is already doing most of this work under load — Romanian deadlifts and hip thrusts train the exact hip extension the tilt is missing, hanging knee raises train anti-extension harder than a dead bug ever will, and split squats stretch the hip flexor on one side while the other works.

So after unassisted pull-ups, drop to:

  • Full routine — twice a week, on your two rest days. It's a nice thing to do on a day you're not lifting.
  • Hip flexor stretch + tall standing reset — daily, two minutes.
  • Tall standing reset in the wild — 3× a day, forever. This one never stops.

You never need to do 12 minutes a day again once the barbell work is doing the heavy lifting. Right now, with no equipment, it's the best use of ten minutes you have.

On the week you missed

Nothing has been undone. Posture work is motor learning, not fitness — you're teaching a default position, and skills decay far slower than conditioning. A week off through illness costs you a couple of sessions of feeling rusty. Pick it up exactly where you left it. Don't add extra sessions to compensate; that's the impulse that turns a missed week into a quit month.

On the tilt

I can't diagnose from photographs and won't pretend otherwise. But I can tell you what the photos are consistent with, and more usefully, what your own second photo already proved.

What the pictures show

Standing relaxed, there's a mild forward tilt of the pelvis with the lower abdomen carried forward — a lumbar curve slightly deeper than neutral. That is mild anterior pelvic tilt, and it is unremarkable. Somewhere around a large majority of adults who sit at a desk measure some degree of it. It is a position, not an injury.

A good part of what reads as tilt in the photos isn't tilt at all — it's a relaxed abdominal wall over a normal amount of subcutaneous fat, photographed standing at ease. Those two things look nearly identical in a mirror and are completely different problems. The first is a habit. The second is answered by the nutrition section of the main plan over months, not by any exercise.

The thing that actually matters

Your second photo is the whole answer

You pushed the hips forward and the stomach tucked in by itself. That means you can voluntarily control your pelvic position — which tells you this is postural and motor-control driven, not structural. Structural tilt doesn't move when you ask it to. Yours moved.

That is genuinely good news, and it means the routine you're already doing is aimed at exactly the right target. Nothing needs to be escalated.

A self-test worth doing once

Two minutes, no equipment, and it gives you a real number instead of a mirror impression.

  1. Stand with your back flat against a wall, heels about 5 cm out, shoulders and head touching.
  2. Stand normally. Don't correct anything.
  3. Slide a flat hand into the gap at your lower back.
  • Palm slides in snugly, not much more — normal. Nothing to fix.
  • Palm plus a lot of room, or your whole fist — meaningful tilt. Worth the ten minutes a day you're already spending.
  • No gap at all — the opposite pattern, also fine.

Do it once. Do it again in three months. Don't do it weekly — you'll just make yourself anxious about noise.

What actually changes it

In order of how much they matter:

  1. How you stand and sit for the other fifteen hours. This is 80% of it, and it's why the standing reset moved out of the routine and into your day.
  2. Hip flexor length and glute strength. Your routine covers both. Glute bridges now, hip thrusts and RDLs later.
  3. Anti-extension core control. Dead bugs now, hanging knee raises later.
  4. Losing abdominal fat. Slow, and mostly a nutrition question.

You are already doing the first three. There's nothing to add beyond the towel extension. Don't go looking for more posture exercises — the internet will happily sell you a forty-minute daily protocol for a problem you're already solving with ten.

And you noticed something real: pull-ups do make you stand taller. Loaded hanging decompresses the spine, and the lats and mid-back you're building are the muscles that hold a tall chest without you having to think about it. The pull-up block is posture work.

Important — this one is easy to get wrong

Weight, while you climb

The main plan tells you to eat +250 kcal and gain to 70 kg. Do not start that yet.

A pull-up is the one lift where your bodyweight is the load. Every kilogram you add makes every remaining rung measurably harder — you'd be climbing a ladder while someone raises it. Gaining 3 kg over a five-month pull-up block would cost you roughly a full rung of progress for nothing.

On doing only pull-ups until then

You're right that front-loading the hardest thing is smart, and I'm not going to argue you out of it. Two things to know so it's a clear-eyed choice rather than a hopeful one: you'll arrive at Phase 0 with a strong back and no pressing or leg base, which is exactly what Phase 0 exists to build — so nothing is wasted. And the four to six months is real time, not a rounding error. You've said you're fine with that. It's a defensible trade and it's yours to make.
