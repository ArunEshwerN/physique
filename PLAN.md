# Lean, not large — a home training program

A twelve-month plan for a specific build: wide shoulders, a full upper chest, lat width,
visible abs. One adjustable bench, two dumbbells, a pull-up bar, 64 kg of cast iron.
Four sessions a week, none longer than an hour.

| | |
|---|---|
| Starting weight | 64.5 kg |
| Target weight | 68–70 kg |
| Target body fat | 12–15% |
| Frequency | 4 days/week |
| Session length | 45–55 min |
| Total kit spend | ₹14,774 |

---

## The honest read

At 64.5 kg having never lifted, getting bulky is not a risk you need to manage — it is the
opposite problem. The reference physiques sit around 10–13% body fat with real shoulder,
upper-chest and lat mass. Daniel Craig in *Casino Royale* was roughly 78 kg at 178 cm.

The 70 kg ceiling is sensible and achievable, and you will not look blocky there. But
understand the direction of travel: hitting that look **requires** getting to roughly that
number. The cap stays. The gaining is not optional.

**Working assumption:** height taken as 168–175 cm. Under 165 cm, shift the target band to
63–66 kg. Over 178 cm, allow 72 kg. Nothing else changes.

### What the look actually requires

Strip away the lighting and every reference photo has the same four things: **side delts,
a full upper chest, lat width, and low enough body fat to see the abs.** Arms matter. Legs
are essentially invisible. So the program is upper-body-biased, with legs trained enough for
health, proportion and injury resistance — but never chased.

## Equipment

### Bars

**Buy two 45 cm threaded bars (ID 8766096), ₹999 each.** Not the 35 cm.

The reason is safety, not capacity. The 35 cm bar relies on Smart Disc Collars, rated
**15 kg in a vertical position**. Every overhead press, incline press and lateral raise is a
vertical position, and you will pass 15 kg per dumbbell inside four months. The threaded
bar's **locknuts thread onto the bar itself**: 30 kg per side, no such limit.

**Skip 50 mm Olympic.** Thick handles, a 5–7 kg empty bar, enormous plates. For dumbbell-only
training at 70 kg bodyweight it is not a limitation you will ever meet. On "19 inch" — 45 cm
is 17.7", already the upper end of comfortable; longer bars clip your ribcage on bench press
and your ears overhead.

### Plates

**Cast iron only. Never 10 kg or 20 kg plates for dumbbells.** The 10 kg disc is 295 mm across
and the 20 kg is 360 mm — on a dumbbell the plate hangs to your knee on a row and hits the
bench on a press. Cast iron 5 kg is 230 mm against rubber's 252 mm: denser, more compact.

## Phase 0 — Weeks 1–4: Foundation

You've never lifted. The biggest cause of quitting in month one is destroying yourself in
week one. So the first four weeks are deliberately, almost frustratingly easy.

> **Do not add extra sets. Do not go to failure.** You should leave every session feeling like
> you could have done twice as much. That feeling is the point. Tendons adapt roughly three
> times slower than muscle — this month is for them.

### Full Body — Monday / Wednesday / Friday

| # | Exercise | Sets × Reps | Rest | Week 1 |
|--:|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Goblet Squat — one DB vertical at the chest, sit down between your knees, heels flat | 2 × 10 | 90 s | 8.3 kg |
| 2 | Flat DB Bench Press — bench 0°, feet planted, elbows ~45° from torso | 2 × 10 | 90 s | 6.3 kg |
| 3 | One-Arm DB Row — knee and hand on bench, pull to the hip not the armpit | 2 × 10/side | 60 s | 8.3 kg |
| 4 | Seated DB Shoulder Press — bench 80°, press to just short of lockout | 2 × 10 | 90 s | 4.3 kg |
| 5 | DB Romanian Deadlift — soft knees, hips *back*, stop at mid-shin | 2 × 10 | 90 s | 8.3 kg |
| 6 | Assisted Chin-Up or Negative | 2 × 5 | 90 s | BW |
| 7 | Plank — straight line head to heels | 2 × 20–30 s | 45 s | BW |

### Week by week

| Week | Change |
|---|---|
| 1 | Weights above, 2 sets |
| 2 | Same weights, **3 sets** |
| 3 | **+2 kg** on every lift, back to 2 sets |
| 4 | Same weights, 3 sets |

### Chin-up progression — follow in order

1. **Dead hang.** Shoulders pulled down, not shrugged. 3 × 20 s. Stay here until you can hang 45 s.
2. **Negatives.** Step or jump to the top, lower as slowly as you can. Target 5 s down. 2 × 5.
3. **Band-assisted chin-ups.** 2 × 5.
4. **Bodyweight chin-ups.**

Most people get their first one in months two to four.

## Warm-up — six minutes, every session

**Upper days**

1. Arm circles — 15 forward, 15 back
2. Band or towel pull-aparts — 10
3. Scapular pull-ups — 10 (hang, shrug down, no elbow bend)
4. Push-ups — 10
5. **First exercise only:** one set of 8 at 50%, one set of 5 at 75%. Then start counting sets.

**Lower days**

1. Bodyweight squats — 20
2. Leg swings — 10 per leg, front to back
3. Unloaded hip hinges — 10
4. **First exercise only:** one set of 8 at 50%, one set of 5 at 75%.

No static stretching before lifting. Save it for after, or for rest days.

## Progression

Every exercise has a **rep range** and a **set count**. You climb the reps, then add weight and
start again. That's the whole method.

```
Week 1:  12 kg × 8, 8, 8
Week 2:  12 kg × 9, 9, 8
Week 3:  12 kg × 10, 10, 9
Week 4:  12 kg × 11, 10, 10
Week 5:  12 kg × 12, 12, 11
Week 6:  12 kg × 12, 12, 12   ← all sets at top of range
Week 7:  14 kg × 8, 8, 8      ← add weight, restart at bottom
```

### The six rules

1. **Pick your starting weight** so you can hit the *bottom* of the range with ~3 reps in
   reserve. If the range is 8–12, pick a weight you could do 11 times — and stop at 8.
2. **Every session, try to add one rep to each set.** Same weight.
3. **Only add weight when *all* sets hit the top of the range** with clean form. Not two out of
   three. All of them.
4. **The jump is +2 kg per dumbbell** (one 1 kg plate per side).
5. **A rep drop after a jump is normal.** Twelve becoming seven is expected. Grind back up.
6. **If you fail to add a rep on any set two sessions running**, drop 4 kg per dumbbell and
   rebuild. This happens maybe twice a year. It's the system working.

### Effort level (RIR = reps in reserve)

| Period | Target RIR | What it feels like |
|---|---:|---|
| Weeks 1–4 (Phase 0) | 3–4 | Comfortably short of failure |
| Weeks 5–10 | 2–3 | Hard but clean |
| Weeks 11–16 | 1–2 | The last rep is a genuine grind |
| Isolation (laterals, curls, calves) | 0–1 | Close to failure — they're safe to |

> **Safety — no rack, no spotter.** Never take DB bench press or overhead press to true failure.
> If you can't complete a rep, don't fight it — let the dumbbells drop out to the sides as you
> sit up. Practise this once with a light weight.

## Blocks and deloads

**Every eight weeks, take a deload week:** same exercises, half the sets, 60% of the weight.
You'll feel like you're wasting a week. You are not — this is where tendons catch up and
fatigue clears. The people who skip deloads are the people who get hurt in month seven.

| Weeks | What you run |
|---|---|
| 1–4 | **Phase 0** — Foundation, 3 days full body |
| 5–16 | **Block 1** — Upper/Lower as written |
| 17 | Deload |
| 18–29 | **Block 2** — same structure, swapped exercises |
| 30 | Deload |
| 31–42 | **Block 1** again, at far heavier weights |

### Block 2 swaps

| Block 1 | Block 2 |
|---|---|
| Flat DB Bench Press | DB Floor Press *or* Deficit Push-Up (hands on DBs) |
| Incline DB Press, 30° | Incline DB Press, **45°** |
| Seated DB Shoulder Press | Arnold Press |
| One-Arm DB Row | DB Meadows-style Row (one DB, staggered stance) |
| Incline DB Curl | Concentration Curl |
| DB Skullcrusher | Overhead DB Triceps Extension |
| Goblet Squat | DB Front-Rack Squat (DBs on shoulders) |
| Bulgarian Split Squat | DB Step-Up (onto the bench) |

This is a plan you can run for five years. The exercises rotate; the method never changes.

## Nutrition, in four rules

You don't need to track. You need four rules and a bathroom scale.

### Rule 1 — Protein: about 120 g/day

Roughly 1.8 g per kg. Don't weigh it, count portions. **One palm-sized serving of a protein
food is about 25–30 g.** You need **four a day**, spread out.

Palm-sized servings: 150 g chicken · 2 eggs + 100 g paneer · 150 g fish · 200 g Greek yoghurt ·
one scoop of whey · 250 g dal with rice (weaker — count as 0.7).

If you're vegetarian this is the hardest part of the plan and whey is worth the money. Two
scoops a day covers half your target.

### Rule 2 — Gain slowly: 0.5–0.75 kg per month

Roughly **+250 kcal above maintenance** — one extra meaningful snack a day. A handful of nuts
and a banana. A glass of full-fat milk with peanut butter. That's it.

Gaining faster than 1 kg/month at your training age adds fat, not muscle — and fat is precisely
what stands between you and those photos. At this rate: **64.5 kg → 70 kg in roughly 9–11
months**, and as a total beginner training four days a week, most of it will be muscle.

### Rule 3 — The weigh-in protocol

Weigh yourself **every morning**: after the bathroom, before eating, same conditions. Daily
weight swings 1–2 kg from water and food — ignore individual days. Every Sunday, average the
week's seven numbers. **That average is your only real data point.**

Checked once a month:

| 4-week average change | Action |
|---|---|
| < 0.3 kg | Add one more snack per day |
| 0.3 – 0.9 kg | **Perfect. Change nothing.** |
| > 1.2 kg | Remove one snack per day |

### Rule 4 — When to stop gaining

Two triggers, whichever comes first:

- You hit **70 kg**, or
- Your **abs stop being visible in normal light** — not flexed, not in a mirror at a good angle,
  just standing there

When either happens, stop adding calories and **hold your weight flat for 4–8 weeks** while you
keep training. You'll keep getting stronger and slowly leaner.

If you've added noticeable fat, run a **mini-cut**: drop ~400 kcal/day for 6–8 weeks, keep
protein at 120 g, and keep lifting the *exact same program at the exact same weights*. Lose
0.4–0.5 kg per week, no faster. Then return to maintenance.

Slow gain → hold → short cut → slow gain. That cycle is how you live in the 12–15% range
permanently instead of yo-yoing.

### Cardio, steps and sleep

- **8,000–10,000 steps a day.** The most underrated tool for staying lean without dieting hard,
  and it costs you no recovery.
- Two easy 30-minute walks, Wednesday and Saturday.
- **No running, no HIIT.** They eat recovery and buy little at your bodyweight. If you enjoy
  them, cap at one a week and put it on Saturday.
- **Sleep 7.5–8 hours.** Non-negotiable. Given the choice between an extra workout and an extra
  hour of sleep — sleep.

cannot run double progression from memory. **The log is the program.**

## Timeline

| When | What to expect |
|---|---|
| Month 1 | Movements start feeling natural. Little visible change. Normal. |
| Month 3 | ~66 kg. First unassisted chin-ups. Shoulders visibly rounder. Clothes fit differently. |
| Month 6 | ~67–68 kg. Upper-chest shelf appears. Lat spread visible from behind. Bench ~20 kg/hand. |
| Month 9–11 | ~70 kg. This is where people start commenting. |
| **Month 12–14** | Hold weight or a short mini-cut → **68–70 kg at ~12–13%. This is the photo.** |
| Year 2–3 | Same bodyweight, better everywhere. Bench 26–30 kg/hand. |

Two things determine whether you get there, and neither is exercise selection: **did you train
four times a week for a hundred weeks**, and **did you log every session so the progression was
real rather than imagined.** Everything above is built so both stay easy. Nothing in it should
ever leave you too wrecked to show up on Thursday.
