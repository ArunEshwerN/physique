# Block Zero — assisted to unassisted pull-ups

Everything in [PLAN.md](PLAN.md) waits until the first clean unassisted pull-up. Until then
this is the whole program: three sessions a week, one rung of band assistance at a time, and
every rung cleared **twice** before you touch the next one.

The interactive tracker lives at **[pullups.html](pullups.html)** — it holds your session log,
your current rung, and a projected unassisted date that moves with your real pace.

---

## The rules

1. **The pain screen runs first, every time.** Three easy scapular reps before the warm-up.
   Anything sharp in the elbow, shoulder or wrist ends the session there — no debate, no
   negotiating with yourself at the bar. A muscle that burns is fine. A joint that pings is not.
2. **Every rep stage gets cleared twice.** Two clean sessions to advance a stage, all stages to
   clear a rung. Single-clearance ladders produce exactly the failure you described on Mark 10.
3. **A new rung restarts at 3/3/3.** Dropping band assistance raises the load, so the volume
   drops to meet it. Reps never carry across a band change.
4. **One cue only: clap the armpits.** Never swap cues mid-set, never stack two.
5. **A scraped rep voids the session.** Not a punishment — information. Log it, repeat it.
6. **Three sessions a week, Mon/Wed/Fri.** Never two pull-up sessions on consecutive days.
7. **If a rung runs fourteen sessions past its minimum**, go back up one mark and re-clear it.
   You lose two weeks and gain the rep quality that was missing.

## The rep stages

Every rung starts at the bottom and climbs **one rep at a time**:

| Stage | Reps | Total |
|---:|---|---:|
| 1 | 3 / 3 / 3 | 9 |
| 2 | 4 / 3 / 3 | 10 |
| 3 | 4 / 4 / 3 | 11 |
| 4 | 4 / 4 / 4 | 12 |
| 5 | 5 / 4 / 4 | 13 |
| 6 | 5 / 5 / 4 | 14 |
| 7 | 5 / 5 / 5 | 15 |

Each stage held twice → **14 sessions per rung**, about 4.5 weeks at three a week. That is
deliberately slow, and it's the cost of the "clear everything twice" rule you asked for.

The tracker has two shorter presets if you ever want to compress — **Standard** (5 stages, 10
sessions per rung) and **Fast** (3 stages, 6 sessions per rung). The default stays Gradual.

> **On the eleven-day break.** You noticed the cue took a few sets to come back while the
> strength was still there. Worth knowing permanently: **after a layoff the skill returns before
> the strength was ever lost.** Re-enter at the same rung and the same reps. Never panic-restart.

## The session — 30 minutes, Mon/Wed/Fri

### 0. Pain screen (30 s) — never skip

**3 easy scapular pull-ups at your easiest mark.** Slow, no effort, purely diagnostic.

Anything sharp in the elbow, shoulder or wrist and the session ends there — do the posture
routine instead and try again next slot. A muscle that burns is fine. A joint that pings is not.

This runs *before* the warm-up, not after. The point is to find out whether today is a training
day before you've spent any energy deciding that it is.

### 1. Warm-up (~4 min)

- **Y-T-W raises, lying** — 5 each. Forehead down or lightly rested, do not lift the head.
  Thumbs up, arms barely off the floor, shoulder blades move gently, no neck strain. Y and T may
  feel like almost nothing — that's fine. W will feel strongest.
- **Arm circles** — 15 forward, 15 backward
- Rest 60 s

### 2. Assisted scapular pull-ups — Mark 10

- Set 1: 5 reps · rest 60 s
- Set 2: 5 reps · rest 90 s

Elbows stay straight. The joint will *appear* to move because the whole shoulder girdle
travels — that's not a bend. Tiny body rise only.

### 3. Assisted pull-ups — current rung

- Set 1: per current stage · rest 2.5 min
- Set 2: per current stage · rest 2.5 min
- Set 3: per current stage · rest 2 min

One cue: clap armpits. If every rep is beautiful, you still do not add reps today. The stage
advances only after two clean sessions.

### 4. Assisted negatives — Mark 10

*Only if the stool setup is clean.*

- Set 1: 3 reps · rest 2.5 min
- Set 2: 3 reps · rest 2 min

Each rep: step to the top · chin clearly over the bar · hold 1 s · lower for 3 s · no swing ·
chin never touches the bar.

If the setup isn't clean, replace with **assisted top-position practice**: 3 singles, step to
the top, hold 1 s, step down, no descent. Safety beats ego and this costs you nothing.

### 5. Cool-down (~2.5 min)

| | |
|---|---:|
| Easy assisted hang, Mark 10 | 10–15 s |
| Doorway chest stretch, left / right | 20 s each |
| Lat stretch, left / right | 20 s each |
| Cross-body shoulder stretch, left / right | 20 s each |

## The posture routine — 8–12 min

Your routine is good and I'm not rewriting it. It already covers breathing mechanics, pelvic
control, glute activation, anti-extension and hip flexor length — the right five things. One
exercise added, one habit changed.

| Exercise | Dose | Rest |
|---|---|---|
| **90/90 breathing / rib stack** — feet up, knees ~90°, flatten low back gently, exhale like fogging a mirror, inhale without flaring ribs | 5 slow breaths | 15–20 s |
| **Pelvic tilts** — tilt so the low back presses down, relax to neutral. No hard crunch; this is control, not ab training | 1 × 10 slow | 20–30 s |
| **Glute bridges** — tuck the pelvis *first*, lift by squeezing glutes, hold 1 s, lower slowly. Never arch to get higher | 2 × 10 | 30–45 s |
| **Dead bug, easy version** — knees up, low back controlled, one limb at a time. If the back arches, shorten the range | 2 × 5/side | 30–45 s |
| **Thoracic extension over a rolled towel** ← *new* | 60–90 s | 20 s |
| **Half-kneeling hip flexor stretch** — squeeze the *back-leg* glute, tuck slightly, then lean | 30 s/side | 10–20 s |
| **Tall standing reset** | 3 × 20 s | 15–20 s |

**The new one:** roll a towel to about wrist thickness. Lie back over it at mid-back height,
roughly level with the bottom of your shoulder blades. Hands behind the head, ribs down,
breathe. This is what your routine was missing — it opens the upper back so that standing tall
doesn't have to come from arching the lower back.

**Tall standing reset, in detail:** feet hip-width and evenly loaded across big toe, little toe
and heel. Knees soft. Glutes at 10–20%. Belt buckle slightly up, not a hard tuck. Ribs stacked
over pelvis, one exhale to bring them down. Neck long, chin gently back. Shoulders relaxed
wide, not yanked back. Should feel tall and relaxed — never puffed, clenched or forced.

### The habit change — this matters more than the routine

**Take the Tall Standing Reset out of the routine and into your day.** Three times daily,
twenty seconds, whenever you remember. Keep it in the routine too.

Posture is not a strength problem, it's a *default position* problem. Ten minutes on a mat
teaches your body the shape; standing in that shape sixty times a week is what makes it the one
you fall into without thinking. The mat work alone will plateau. This is the part that transfers.

### Short version, when time is tight

- Pelvic tilts — 10
- Glute bridges — 2 × 10
- Hip flexor stretch — 30 s/side
- Tall standing reset — 2 × 20 s

Four minutes, and genuinely enough on a bad day.

### Do you keep it after the main program starts?

**Yes, but it shrinks.** Phase 1 already does most of this under load — RDLs and hip thrusts
train the hip extension the tilt is missing, hanging knee raises train anti-extension harder
than a dead bug ever will, split squats stretch one hip flexor while the other works.

So after unassisted:

- **Full routine** — twice a week, on rest days
- **Hip flexor stretch + tall standing reset** — daily, two minutes
- **Tall standing reset in the wild** — 3× a day, forever

> **On the week you missed:** nothing has been undone. Posture work is *motor learning*, not
> conditioning — skills decay far slower than fitness. Pick it up exactly where you left it, and
> don't add extra sessions to compensate. That impulse is what turns a missed week into a quit
> month.

## On the tilt

I can't diagnose from photographs and won't pretend otherwise. But here's what they're
consistent with, and what your own second photo already proved.

**What the pictures show.** Standing relaxed, there's a mild forward tilt of the pelvis with the
lower abdomen carried forward and a lumbar curve slightly deeper than neutral. That is **mild
anterior pelvic tilt, and it is unremarkable** — a large majority of desk-working adults measure
some. It's a position, not an injury.

A good part of what reads as tilt isn't tilt at all: it's a relaxed abdominal wall over a normal
amount of subcutaneous fat, photographed standing at ease. Those look nearly identical in a
mirror and are completely different problems. The first is a habit. The second is answered by
the nutrition section over months, not by any exercise.

**Your second photo is the whole answer.** You pushed the hips forward and the stomach tucked in
by itself. That means you can *voluntarily control your pelvic position* — so this is postural
and motor-control driven, not structural. Structural tilt doesn't move when you ask it to.
Yours moved. Genuinely good news, and it means your routine is aimed at the right target.

### A self-test worth doing once

1. Stand with your back flat against a wall, heels ~5 cm out, shoulders and head touching.
2. Stand normally. Don't correct anything.
3. Slide a flat hand into the gap at your lower back.

- **Palm slides in snugly, not much more** — normal. Nothing to fix.
- **Palm plus a lot of room, or your whole fist** — meaningful tilt. Worth the ten minutes a day.
- **No gap at all** — the opposite pattern, also fine.

Do it once. Do it again in three months. Not weekly — you'll just make yourself anxious about noise.

### What actually changes it, in order

1. **How you stand and sit for the other fifteen hours.** 80% of it.
2. **Hip flexor length and glute strength.** Covered — bridges now, hip thrusts and RDLs later.
3. **Anti-extension core control.** Dead bugs now, hanging knee raises later.
4. **Losing abdominal fat.** Slow, and mostly nutrition.

You're already doing the first three. **Don't go looking for more posture exercises** — the
internet will sell you a forty-minute daily protocol for a problem you're already solving in ten.

And you noticed something real: pull-ups *do* make you stand taller. Hanging decompresses the
spine, and the lats and mid-back you're building are the muscles that hold a tall chest without
you thinking about it. The pull-up block is posture work.

## Weight, while you climb

The main plan says eat +250 kcal and gain to 70 kg. **Do not start that yet.**

A pull-up is the one lift where your bodyweight *is* the load. Every kilogram added makes every
remaining rung measurably harder — climbing a ladder while someone raises it. Gaining 3 kg over
a five-month pull-up block would cost you roughly a full rung for nothing.

| Rule | During Block Zero | After unassisted |
|---|---|---|
| Protein | ~120 g/day | ~120 g/day |
| Calories | **Maintenance — hold weight flat** | +250 kcal |
| Target weight | 64–65 kg, steady | → 68–70 kg |
| Weigh-in | Every morning, weekly average | Every morning, weekly average |
| Steps | 8,000–10,000/day | 8,000–10,000/day |

Keep protein high — that's what protects the muscle you're building on the bar. Just don't add
the surplus. **The gaining phase starts the week you get your first clean unassisted rep.**

**On doing only pull-ups until then.** Front-loading the hardest thing is smart and I'm not
arguing you out of it. Two things so it's a clear-eyed choice: you'll arrive at Phase 0 with a
strong back and no pressing or leg base, which is exactly what Phase 0 exists to build — so
nothing is wasted. And the four-to-six months is real time, not a rounding error. You've said
you're fine with that. It's a defensible trade and it's yours to make.
